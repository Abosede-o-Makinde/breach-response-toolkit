# ICO Notification Guide — Article 33(3) Field-by-Field

> **Status:** Outline — full content to be completed during documentation phase.
> **Audience:** DPOs preparing an ICO breach notification under UK GDPR Article 33.

## Overview

Under Article 33(1), controllers must notify the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach — unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals.

Under Article 33(3), the notification must contain **four specific content fields**. Missing any field can result in enforcement action for inadequate notification.

## The four mandatory fields

### (a) Nature of the breach

**What to include:**

- Description of what happened
- Categories of personal data affected
- Approximate number of data subjects
- Approximate number of records

**Common mistakes:**

- Vague descriptions ("a security incident occurred")
- Missing approximate numbers
- Not listing data categories

### (b) Contact point

**What to include:**

- Name of the DPO or other contact point
- Email address
- Telephone number

**Common mistakes:**

- Out-of-date contact details
- Generic mailbox instead of named contact

### (c) Likely consequences

**What to include:**

- Specific, concrete consequences for affected individuals
- Risk of identity theft, financial fraud, discrimination, etc.

**Common mistakes:**

- Generic statements ("may cause inconvenience")
- No link between data type and consequence

### (d) Measures taken or proposed

**What to include:**

- Containment actions already taken
- Remediation steps planned
- Mitigation for affected individuals

**Common mistakes:**

- Only describing the breach, not the response
- No timeline for proposed measures

The evidence log includes a `article_33_3_completeness_percent` field that scores all four mandatory fields before generating the ICO notification draft.
